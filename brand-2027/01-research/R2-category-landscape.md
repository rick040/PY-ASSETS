Method: homepages captured 2026-11-07 with Playwright (1440x900). Hexes are tagged **CSS** (computed styles or `:root` custom properties), **SVG** (fill in the live logo file) or **eyedrop** (most frequent pixel in a screenshot). Adjacent brands: NS (same Dutch customer, one yellow/blue system across trains, stations and app), TfL (one device, the roundel, from map to platform), Schiphol (benchmark Dutch wayfinding), Citymapper (friendliest digital mobility tone), Swapfiets (one product detail works as the logo). ### Key findings
- **The category is consolidating.** apcoa.nl now redirects to a Q-Park page saying Q-Park took over APCOA Nederland, with sites split between Q-Park and P1. P1 has been a Q-Park company since 2019. Parkmobile became EasyPark in January 2023. EasyPark and Yellowbrick now sit under Arrive (June 2025). OPnGO is now "Indigo Neo". Fewer, bigger brands are left.
- **Navy is the operator default.** Interparking #031B56, APCOA #001E46, P1 #282359 and ParkingYou #1A3580 all build on dark blue; Q-Park uses near-black #1C1F29. A letter P as the logo is rarer than I assumed (only P1). At the garage door, though, the blue regulatory P panel (RVV E4, assumption) is used by everyone. Q-Park's and Indigo's own photos show a blue P with only a small logo cap above it. The P is shared infrastructure; it does not tell operators apart.
- **The yellow/green hypothesis is half wrong.** Yellow is crowded: ParkBee #FCD136, Yellowbrick #FED303, Interparking accent #FCD116, NS #FFC917 and Schiphol's yellow signs. Green is not really taken: Q-Park's logo is black and red #ED1C2E, and green #8BB611 is only its web button. EasyPark has been purple/pink since its October 2022 identity, not yellow.
- **Other colour families are spoken for.** Red belongs to the Q-Park group (Q-Park, P1). Purple and magenta belong to EasyPark and Indigo. Orange is APCOA's accent #F7941C, which sits close to PY-Oranje #F08300; that overlap shrinks now that APCOA has left NL. **No Dutch operator leads with aqua or teal.** The nearest are APCOA's small cyan #00AEEF and Swapfiets' #00A9E0, which is outside parking.
- **Diagonals are the category's shorthand for movement:** Q-Park's slashes, P1's 130° wedges, Interparking's 128° yellow corner and APCOA's angled header. Round geometry is almost unused. The exception is Indigo's magenta pin-O, a direct neighbour to the pin reading of ParkingYou's beeldmerk.
- **Type is geometric or neutral sans:** Futura (Q-Park, Indigo), Inter (P1, EasyPark), Roboto, Montserrat, Poppins. Nobody else uses Ubuntu or a humanist face.
- **The best adjacent systems are built on one device.** TfL's roundel, NS's yellow field and Swapfiets' blue tyre each carry the brand from a small screen to large scale. Schiphol separates brand from function: sign families are colour-coded by task. None of the operator homepages showed a motion language when captured (assumption: we saw static captures only).

### What this means for ParkingYou
- **Lead with aqua and anchor with navy.** Aqua as a large field with PY-Blauw text (4.80:1) is the one colour position no Dutch operator holds. Navy alone would merge into Interparking, P1 and the regulatory P.
- **Own round geometry.** The bubble, circle, pill and arc are the opposite of the category's diagonals. Push the speech-bubble reading (conversation, "menselijk wanneer het moet") and play down the pin reading, which sits close to Indigo and to every app map (assumption).
- **Keep orange as a small signal and stay out of yellow.** Yellow is taken by ParkBee and Yellowbrick, and partner marks (EasyPark purple, ParkBee yellow) already sit on ParkingYou terminals.
- **Aim for the empty quadrant: friendly, but as disciplined as TfL or Schiphol.** One device across every scale, plus sign families coded by task, not by decoration.
- **Make Ubuntu a deliberate signature.** It is unique in the category and free. For photography, avoid AI lifestyle (EasyPark) and generic skylines (APCOA); real ParkingYou garages and people would stand out (assumption).
